### Wed Nov 11: What is AI actually good at in an office?
> Owners keep asking me: "What is AI actually good at in an office like mine?"
>
> Five honest answers:
>
> 1. Reading documents. Invoices, purchase orders, contracts. It pulls out the details so nobody retypes them.
> 2. Moving information between programs. Type it once, it shows up everywhere.
> 3. First drafts. Replies, quotes, reminders. A person checks and sends.
> 4. Answering questions from your own data. "Who owes us more than $10,000?"
> 5. Doing the same task, the same way, every time. At 2am if needed.
>
> And what it's not good at: replacing the judgment of people who know your business.
>
> That's the point. Let the computer do the copy-paste. Let your people do the work that matters.
>
> #AI #SmallBusiness #Automation

### Fri Nov 13: When your bookkeeper retires
> What happens to your business when your bookkeeper retires?
>
> In a lot of established companies, one or two long-time people hold the whole back office in their heads.
> Which vendor gets paid when. Which customer always pays late. How the month-end report gets built.
>
> When they leave, all of that walks out the door.
>
> The fix isn't hiring a replacement and hoping. It's getting the process out of their head and into a system:
> - Write down the steps while they're still here
> - Automate the repetitive parts
> - Keep a person in charge of the decisions
>
> You end up with a back office that runs on systems, not memory. And a business that's worth more.
>
> #SuccessionPlanning #FamilyBusiness #Automation

## Week 2 posts

### Mon Nov 16: The gaps between programs
> Your old software isn't the problem.
>
> I hear owners apologize for running QuickBooks Desktop, or a program from 2009, like it's something to be
> embarrassed about. It's not. It works. Your team knows it.
>
> The real problem is the gaps between your programs. That's where people spend their day:
> copying orders from email into the job system, retyping customers into accounting, exporting spreadsheets to
> build a report.
>
> Close the gaps and the old software suddenly works like new. No migration. No retraining.
>
> That's most of what I do at TailorWired.
>
> #Automation #QuickBooks #SmallBusiness

### Wed Nov 18: Is it safe to let AI near my financial data?
> "Is it safe to let AI near my financial data?"
>
> It's the right question. Here's how I answer it for every client:
>
> → Each automation only gets access to what it needs. Nothing more.
> → You can see everything that's connected, and switch it off any time.
> → Anything that sends money or messages a customer can require a person's OK first.
> → Your data stays yours.
>
> Good automation should make your business more controlled, not less.
>
> #AI #DataSecurity #SmallBusiness

### Fri Nov 20: Systems a buyer will pay more for
> Thinking about selling your business someday, or handing it to the next generation?
>
> Buyers pay more for businesses that run without the owner in every decision.
>
> Things that make a buyer nervous:
> - "Only Linda knows how to do payroll"
> - Numbers that take two weeks to pull together
> - Processes that live in someone's head
>
> Things that make a buyer confident:
> - Clean, current books
> - Reports that run on their own
> - Documented, automated processes
>
> You don't need to overhaul everything. Start with the one process that would cause the biggest headache if
> the person running it left tomorrow.
>
> #ExitPlanning #FamilyBusiness #Automation
